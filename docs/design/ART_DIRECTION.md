## The One Rule
**Every scene has a story told in light: ONE motivated key, ONE accent color, real darkness
between.** AAA doesn't mean bright — it means *deliberate*. If a surface is lit, something
lights it. If a color sings, it sings alone. Flat even illumination is forbidden everywhere.

## The Global Palette
- **Bone** — the facility's white concrete / panel base (warm off-white, never pure #fff)
- **Void** — near-black blues (shadow, night sky, unlit depth) — darkness is a color we USE
- **Signal cyan** — technology speaking: holos, terminals, VIGIL, the elevator crest
- **Crystal spectrum** (pink→cyan→blue) — the alien/living: crystals, batteries, the strata's glow
- **Blood amber/red** — danger only: alert states, monsters' domain, KILL SQUAD lighting
- Rule: a scene leads with bone+void, then earns ONE of the three accent families. Two accents
  in frame = a deliberate story beat (e.g., cyan holo vs red lockdown). Three = never.

## Zone Bibles (the level-by-level artistic design)

### 1. Detention (the opening — first 10 minutes = the review score)
- **Story**: sterile control, long inhabited, lately broken.
- Materials: bone concrete panels w/ subtle service wear at hand height, brushed steel door
  frames, matte dark floor with a polished center walk-strip (SSR catches the sconces).
- Light: warm-white sconce pools down the WIDE hall (the canonical layout's spine), cool
  blue-white spill from cell interiors through glass, darkness between pools. Jake's cell:
  one warm ceiling unit + the cyan glow of the corridor holo bleeding under the door.
- Accent: signal cyan (terminals, door keypads). Red exists ONLY when the alert wakes.

### 2. The Main Hall / Security / Armory
- Security: cooler light, monitor-glow blue-green, tighter shadows — surveillance mood.
- Armory: functional amber task lighting over racks; the one warm-lit room on the floor —
  "here is where you become dangerous."
- Hall: the cathedral of the floor — sconce rhythm + a long ceiling light-slot; the widest
  brightest space, so every side door reads as a darker mystery.

### 3. Level 4.5 — THE MONSTER SECTION (70–80 ft void)
- **Story**: the building's secret organ. Industrial-cathedral scale.
- Near-void darkness; thin service catwalk lit by sparse red-amber cage lamps; the far walls
  NEVER fully visible — scale implied by sound (RT acoustics) and silhouette. Crystal-spectrum
  bioluminescence where the biomesh intrudes. The scariest light rig in the game.

### 4. The Strata (the elevator descent + caves)
- **Story**: the earth is a body; we drilled through it.
- Layered rock bands each with material identity (sediment → igneous → the crystal veins);
  the cab's glass frames it like a slow vertical dolly shot. Crystal veins glow pink-cyan,
  intensity rising with depth — the descent literally warms into alien color as Club 1127 nears.

### 5. Club 1127 (the bottom of the world)
- **Story**: hedonism in the deep — the reward for descent.
- Dark polished floor (full SSR mirror), crystal-spectrum neon signage, warm skin-tone key
  light at the bar vs cool rim from the dance floor. The one place BOTH cool accents play
  at once — it's the story beat that earns it.

### 6. Exterior / The Landing (the Lab2.jpg shot)
- Direct translation of the reference: rim-lit bone facade vs starry void, dark rubble
  foreground, crystal clusters as the foreground key. The antenna crown silhouetted.
  Day variant: hard raking sun modeling the spandrel reveals, glass nearly black.

## Craft Standards (enforced on every pass)
1. 2K minimum on hero surfaces; tiling invisible at arm's length (break repeats w/ decals/grime/trim).
2. Normal maps must EARN the sconces — surfaces respond to raking light or get re-sourced.
3. Trim sells construction: baseboards, door frames, panel reveals at every material change (Law 4).
4. Wear tells history: hand-height scuffs, floor traffic paths, drip stains under service pipes —
   placed where life would put them, never noise-uniform.
5. Emissives are light sources, not stickers: every glowing surface casts (point light or GI).
6. THE GATE: every zone pass ends with a screenshot set REVIEWED BY EYE against this bible +
   Lab2.jpg. Score /10. Below 8 iterates. "Would this frame survive in a AAA trailer?" is the question.
