## 0. Why the 2000–2002 window

The 2000 season is the peak-nostalgia snapshot for a Cedar Point replica, and 2000–2002 is a remarkably *stable*
window — only **one** ride changed:

- **2000:** Millennium Force debuts (May 13). The headline of the whole window.
- **2001:** **No new coaster.** Quiet year — Millennium Force in its second season; the resort **Lighthouse Point**
  opened (cottages/cabins/RV, not a ride); the **Cedar Point Cinema** ran its final season and closed after 2001; the
  old **Aquarium / Stadium Games** building was razed **October 2001** to start Wicked Twister construction.
- **2002:** **Wicked Twister** debuts (May 5) — twin 215 ft twisting steel spikes **directly on the beach**, on the
  former Aquarium site. The park's 15th coaster. This is the single visible ride delta across the window.

So a 2000 visitor and a 2002 visitor saw **almost the identical park** — same coasters, same midways, same skyline —
**except** for Wicked Twister on the beach and the loss of the Cinema. That makes the build efficient: one faithful
park, one toggleable 2002 addition.

Why 2000 anchors the look regardless:

- **Millennium Force debuted May 13, 2000** — the world's first "giga" coaster (>300 ft), the park's crown that
  summer, and the single most iconic silhouette on the peninsula. Anchoring on 2000 lets us make it the centerpiece
  while it was brand new.
- **The nostalgia payload — rides that were alive in 2000 and are now GONE.** A faithful 2000 park lets a player walk
  past attractions that no longer exist anywhere: **White Water Landing** (log flume, removed 2005), **Disaster
  Transport** (indoor space-bobsled, demolished 2012), **WildCat** (Schwarzkopf mouse, removed 2011/12), **Demon Drop**
  (removed 2009, relocated), **Space Spiral** (observation tower, removed 2012), **Mean Streak** (wooden monster,
  gutted into Steel Vengeance 2016), **Mantis** (stand-up, converted to Rougarou 2015). Half the emotional value of
  this build is standing next to things that are physically gone from the real world.
- The window's **ceiling is 2002**: **Wicked Twister** (2002) is the *latest* thing that can appear, and only as the
  optional "2002 layer." Everything after is **out** — maXair 2005, Skyhawk 2006, Top Thrill Dragster 2003,
  GateKeeper 2013, Steel Vengeance 2018. None of them exist in our snapshot.

## 2. PARK LAYOUT PLAN — graybox-ready

Cedar Point sits on a **sandy peninsula jutting north into Lake Erie** from Sandusky, Ohio. The park is long and
narrow, threaded by a **main midway spine** running roughly SE→NW, with the **beach and Lake Erie on the east/north
edges** and the marina/bay on the west. The single road/**causeway** enters from the south (mainland).

The engine already has an **ocean/water system** and **terrain** — the lake is a free horizon, and the beach is a
terrain+water reuse. That makes the "peninsula surrounded by water" reading essentially free scenery that also
solves the skybox and reflection story.

## 3. TRACK TECH — what coasters need beyond the descent-slide lane

The in-flight **descent-slide lane** (this branch) is building a **generic spline track + ride controller**: control
points, banking, airtime, and a camera rig. That is ~70% of a coaster system. What coasters add on top:

**New subsystems required:**
1. **Lift-hill segments** — a spline segment tagged `LIFT` with a constant-velocity drive (chain or cable). Millennium
   Force specifically is a **cable lift** (fast, ~elevator feel) vs. Blue Streak/Magnum **chain dog** (clanking,
   slow). Model as a per-segment "drive velocity + drive type" property; the physics on non-lift segments stays pure
   gravity + friction like the slide.
2. **Trains = multi-car linked capsules.** The slide is one rider capsule; a train is N cars rigidly spaced along the
   spline arc-length, each sampling the spline at `s + offset_i`. Car pitch/roll come from the spline frame at each
   car's `s`. This is the single biggest addition and is reusable across every coaster.
3. **Inversions** — the controller "supports full roll," so inversions are just banking that passes through 180°+.
   No new math, but the **spline frame must be parallel-transport / Frenet-with-roll** (not up-vector-locked) or it
   flips at the top of loops. Verify the frame is roll-stable before Phase D.
4. **Block system + station stop** — segments tagged `BLOCK` with occupancy; a `STATION` segment with a scripted
   stop/dwell/dispatch. Minimum viable: one block per train, station brake + dispatch timer. Full multi-train
   blocking is a later polish pass.
5. **Buildings** — queue switchbacks + station structure per ride (GLB props). Reuse the environment/GLB kit; these
   are set dressing, not track tech.

**Build phasing (tech-risk ordered):**

- **Phase A — Blue Streak (the tech prover).** Simple wooden out-and-back, no inversions, chain lift, one train.
  Proves: lift-hill drive, single-train-on-spline, station stop, gravity handoff from lift to free-run. Lowest risk,
  validates the whole coaster loop end to end. **Do this first.**
- **Phase B — Magnum XL-200 (airtime + height).** Still no inversions. Adds: real scale (200 ft lift), sustained
  **airtime hills** (negative-G tuning on the camera + capsule), longer track streaming. Proves the airtime model at
  hyper scale.
- **Phase C — Millennium Force (giga scale + overbanks).** Adds: **cable-lift drive type**, 310 ft streaming/LOD,
  **overbanked turns** (>90° banking without inversion — stresses the roll frame short of a full loop), and the
  water-crossing scenery. Proves scale + banking extremes.
- **Phase D — Raptor (inversions).** Adds: full 6-inversion roll-stable frame, inverted (below-track) train geometry,
  tighter block logic. Highest tech risk — the roll frame and train articulation get their hardest test. Do last.

Multi-car **trains** land in Phase A (Blue Streak needs a train, not one capsule). Everything after reuses it.

## 5. PLACEMENT IN THE GAME WORLD — 2–3 options for Tim to rule on

**No decision made here.** Presented for Tim.

### Option A — Abandoned 2000-condition park (post-abandonment nostalgia) — RECOMMENDED for tone
The park exists in the game world **frozen and decayed** in its 2000 configuration: rusting Millennium Force lift,
faded Camp Snoopy murals, a silent midway, the CP&LE stopped on its track. The player explores a dead park.
- **Pros:** Enormous emotional payload — it leans directly into "rides that no longer exist" and this game's tone.
  No crowd sim, no operating-ride AI needed for the base experience (coasters can be static or ghost-run). Lighting
  and decay do the heavy lifting; graybox → atmosphere is a short path. Strongest fit for EFLZ's mood.
- **Cons:** "Faithful 2000 replica" is partly undercut by decay layers (need both the pristine model AND a wear pass).
  Fewer moments of the rides actually running (though a haunted ghost-train run is very powerful).

### Option B — Functioning park as a live hub/level
The park is **alive** — rides running, trains cycling, a working Millennium Force you can actually ride.
- **Pros:** Maximum wow; the coaster tech from Section 3 pays off as playable rideable coasters; natural social/hub
  space. The "in 2000, brand new" fantasy delivered literally.
- **Cons:** Most expensive — needs full ride-operation logic, block systems, ambient crowd sim, and audio to not feel
  hollow. Highest art+tech cost. Tonally the biggest departure from the game's darker register unless framed carefully.

### Option C — Simulation / memory space inside the facility
The park is a **reconstructed memory or simulation** accessed from within the game's facility — deliberately
imperfect, dreamlike, or glitching at the edges.
- **Pros:** Narrative license to be selective (only build the signature rides), to fade the horizon, to justify
  emptiness or oddness. Cheapest to scope — build the hero corners, let the sim "not render" the rest. Fits a
  facility/lab framing cleanly.
- **Cons:** Undercuts "faithful replica" by design; less of a place, more of a set. Risks feeling like a tech demo
  rather than a beloved location.

**Integrator's lean (not a decision):** **Option A** captures the most value from the least new tech and fits the
game's tone — but it can *contain* a Option-B moment (one ride you can bring back to life / ghost-ride), and could be
framed as Option C narratively. These are not mutually exclusive; A as the base with a single rideable hero coaster is
the strongest blend.

## 6. IP NOTE (practical)

Real-place layouts are standard and widely shipped in games (racing sims, city builders, and countless "inspired-by"
parks). The **geography and layout of a real place are not the sensitive part** — the **trademarked names and logos
are**. Recommendation: build the **faithful 2000 layout and ride geometry**, but for any **public release** rename the
park and its trademarked rides and drop the real wordmarks/character art (Peanuts is separately licensed and should
not ship). Keep an internal **rename map** so the build stays organized while we work from real references:

| Real (reference) | Ship name (example) |
|------------------|---------------------|
| Cedar Point | **The Point** / **Erie Point** |
| Millennium Force | **Force 310** |
| Magnum XL-200 | **Magnitude XL** |
| Raptor | **Talon** |
| Mean Streak | **Grudge** |
| Blue Streak | **Comet Streak** |
| Gemini | **Twins** |
| Camp Snoopy / Peanuts | **Camp [original mascot]** (replace character IP entirely) |

Faithful to the place, clean on the marks. This is a build-time note, not a legal opinion — flag for a real review
before any public ship.

## 7. PIN THE YEAR — memory-joggers for Tim (2000 vs. 2001 vs. 2002)

Tim's visit was **2000 or 2002**. Because the park was nearly identical across the window, a few concrete, *visual*
distinguishers do all the work. Read these as "what would I actually have seen / felt?" — memory, not trivia.

**The single decisive tell — the beach:**
- **Did you see twin white/silver steel spikes twisting straight up into the sky right at the beach — a coaster that
  launched you backward and forward and never did a loop?** → **That's Wicked Twister → you were there in 2002.** It's
  unmistakable and it's right on the sand; you could not miss it.
- **Was that stretch of beach instead an aquarium building / arcade, or a construction wall with dirt and cranes?**
  → **Aquarium standing = 2000.** → **Construction wall / torn-up beach = late 2001** (they razed it that October).

**Millennium Force tells (all three years it's there, but the *feel* differs):**
- **Was Millennium Force the brand-new, everyone's-talking-about-it, two-to-three-hour-line, "world's first giga
  coaster" headline with banners everywhere?** → strongly **2000** (its debut summer). By 2002 it was beloved but no
  longer the shrieking-new novelty.

**Other quiet tells:**
- **Do you remember the Cedar Point Cinema (a movie theater in the park)?** If you actually watched something there, it
  was open **2000 or 2001** — it closed after the 2001 season, so a **2002** visit couldn't include it.
- **Peanuts everywhere in the kids' area (Camp Snoopy, Woodstock Express, Snoopy statues)?** Present all three years
  (debuted 1999) — this doesn't distinguish, but its *absence* would mean pre-1999, ruling the memory out of window.
- **Count the "tallest coasters": if the tallest thing you rode was Millennium Force and there was nothing taller or
  newer than it**, that's consistent with 2000/2001. Wicked Twister (2002) is shorter than MF but is the *newest* and
  the one on the beach — its presence is the clean 2002 signal.

**Bottom line for the build:** if Tim can't decide, build the **2000-default** park and keep **Wicked Twister + the
Aquarium removal** on a single toggle. Flipping that one switch *is* the 2000↔2002 difference for a visitor.
