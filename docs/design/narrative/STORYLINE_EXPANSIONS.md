# EFLZ — Storyline Expansions (six directions)

Each treatment plugs into the existing structure (EFLZ_NARRATIVE / MASTER_PLAN /
WORLD_STRUCTURE / BESTIARY / X3_WORLD_BLUEPRINT) without contradicting it. Where a
treatment touches an existing beat, the touch is *additive recontextualization* — the beat
plays the same the first time; the expansion changes what it meant. Flags referenced are
defined in NPC_CHAT_TREE_FORMAT.md §7 and set by the chat_trees/ pack.

---

## (a) THE QUARTERMASTER — who shot Jake down, and why six months

**The thread.** The cold open (INTRO_COLD_OPEN.md) is currently an unexplained ambush: Jake
flies ~45 seconds, a much bigger ship fires one energy pulse, fade, six months, cell. This
expansion makes the pulse a *purchase order*.

**The truth, layered out across the acts:**
1. **The shot was a tractor-harvest, not a kill.** A single disabling pulse from a Dominion
   freight hull — the grey line that supplies Lab Zero. Big ships don't dogfight; they
   *collect*. First hint: Martinez's pre-fight line ("Pilot. Shot down, salvaged,
   stabilized") establishes within minutes that the facility's own paperwork calls Jake
   salvage.
2. **The targeting was human.** Dominion hulls can't read human transponders and flight
   lanes; their procurement broker can. **The Quartermaster** — human, well-dressed,
   smells like cold metal and good cologne (Vesper's tell-scene) — sells the program its
   groceries: fuel, parts, *acquisitions*. He flagged Jake's ship for collection. Vesper
   opens the thread (`quartermaster.known` → quest `quartermaster_ledger`): his ledger
   physically rides the freight pipe down twice a year, and the next run is soon — an
   Act-1-tail / Act-2 heist beat in a space the engine already has (the descent
   tube/freight line).
3. **Why Jake specifically.** The ledger's entry for Jake's collection is *pre-dated* —
   the order was cut **before** his flight plan was filed. Cross-corroborated by Hollow
   Pete's queue reveal (`jake_scheduled`: 7-Alpha's augmentation chair was reserved before
   he woke) and Emily's harvest reveal (`harvest.known`): the program had exactly one
   genome on record that retains free will through integration — the command-caste
   keystone — and they'd been *shopping for its carrier*. Jake's genetic anomaly was
   known to someone with access to human medical databases. The Quartermaster didn't find
   Jake's ship. He found Jake's *bloodwork*.
4. **Why alive six months.** Not imprisonment — **harvesting** (Emily's ledger
   conclusion). Phase 1 enhancement is the cover story the monitor tells; the real six
   months were: genome extraction for the Clone's gestation (the Clone is *grown from the
   harvest*, which is why he's "optimized" — he's Jake with the will surgically absent),
   plus anomaly-strand seeding into the gestation cohorts — which is why **everything the
   program builds after Jake has a Jake-shaped flaw**. The payoff is mechanical as well as
   narrative: late-game, `harvest.known` can justify a damage/weakness rule against
   hybrid-caste enemies (the Bestiary's Stage-3+/officer types).
5. **The payoff.** The Quartermaster is a recurring *civilian* antagonist — never a boss
   fight in Act 1; he's a ledger, a cologne, an empty barstool. He surfaces in person in
   Act 2 (Spaceport, brokering the Garrison's resupply — optional confrontation), and his
   network IS the seed of Act 4's Human Collaborators (canon faction), making the
   collaborator arc personal: Earth wasn't infiltrated by aliens first; it was *sold* by
   men with ledgers. Final beat option: his ledger, captured, is the evidence package
   (with Reyes's batch records and Aria's manifest) that powers the collaborator trials in
   the high-karma endings. Martinez (if talked down / spared) is the natural prosecutor —
   "I know EXACTLY whose names go in it."

**Canon fit.** Touches nothing established: the cold open plays identically; Dominion =
canon builders of Lab Zero; collaborators = canon Act-4 faction; the Clone's "extra
enhancements" (bestiary) now have an origin story; the 12 endings unaffected (the trials
ride inside Golden/Good/Chen's-Redemption epilogues).

## (b) THE CRADLE PROTOCOL — what the impregnation program is FOR

**The gap being filled.** Canon says the program breeds "a hybrid invasion army adapted to
Earth's atmosphere" (EFLZ_NARRATIVE §1). True — but it under-explains the Bestiary: why do
hybrids come in *castes* (Stage 1 feral → Stage 3 commander with telepathic summon → the
Overseer's psychic command tier)? And why does a hive that owns 2,847 worlds need wombs at
all — why not vats (it HAS vats — Floor 3's tanks are full of failures)?

**The answer: the program isn't breeding soldiers. It's breeding OFFICERS.**
- The Overlord's hive can mass-produce obedience (Verthani soldiery, drone swarms — the
  vat-grown trash tiers). What it cannot manufacture is **initiative**: a node that can be
  trusted to *decide* at the far end of a light-lag, cut off from the hive chorus. Every
  vat attempt produces either a puppet (useless alone) or a defector (Failed Experiment
  #7's lineage — wills that snap back). The Floor-3 tanks are the fossil record of this
  problem.
- Human gestation is the missing manufacturing step. Carried hybrids — grown inside a
  willful host — integrate the *capacity for will* without (usually) the will itself.
  The mothers aren't incubators by sadism; the womb is the only bioreactor that builds
  the command lobe. This is why the program centers on **women, held and impregnated**
  — the horror has a cold industrial logic, which makes it more horrifying, not less
  (and keeps the content's meaning where Tim's docs put it: forced breeding as the
  ultimate violation of autonomy — the program literally *mines autonomy*).
- **The caste map onto the Bestiary:** vat tiers = Stage 1–2 / drones / Verthani;
  carried tiers = Stage 3 Hybrid commanders, the Overseer line, the Illuminated
  ("energy-being elite" = the program's perfected output, generations ahead); and the
  designed apex — a carried hybrid from a *free-will-retaining* line — is what Jake's
  harvested anomaly (thread a) finally enables. The Clone assaulting Sarah on F7 is not
  lust or cruelty: it is the program's **final assembly step** — anomaly-bearing father,
  selected mother ("the Bride suite," Reyes's line) — the first true Officer. Stopping it
  isn't just saving Sarah; it's aborting the enemy's end-state. (Gamma's "The Bride"
  becomes precisely this: the program completing without Jake.)
- **Late-act payoff.** The L45 "Return to Lab Zero" sub-level reveal (Breeding Program
  Core, 47,000 processed) and the 65-million-year Original Artifact slot under this
  cleanly: the Overlord has been running Cradle-class programs since before mammals —
  Earth itself is a *long-cycle cradle world*, seeded and left to ripen. The dinosaur-
  killer pod wasn't an attack; it was a planting. Humanity is the harvest the Overlord
  came back for — which is why it conquers via gifts first (canon): you don't burn the
  orchard you grew.

**Canon fit.** Pure deepening: every named beat (tanks, castes, Clone/Sarah, Bride,
Artifact, gifts-first doctrine) already exists; this gives them one spine. The name
"Cradle" enters only through dialog (Emily, Reyes — `cradle.known`), so docs needn't
retcon anything.

## (c) THE CARRIER — a betrayal/mole arc among rescued companions (optional, discoverable)

**Premise.** A girl rescued at `InfectionStage::Critical` (the 30%-cure window — the
timeline.h stage machinery already models this) survives *carrying a dormant strand*. Not
a traitor: a **compromised channel**. The hive can't command her — the strand never
finished — but it can *listen through her*, and sometimes, shallowly, whisper.

**Default carrier: Lena** (her tree ships the full arc), but the design is roster-generic:
the host sets `<girl>.carrier` for whoever was rescued latest/at Critical, so the arc can
land on Aria/Keisha/Emily in other playthroughs — and what you saved her FROM changes what
the whispers use (Aria: patient names; Keisha: cadence calls; Emily: her own narration).

**Discovery (player-driven, never forced):** three breadcrumb classes, escalating —
1. *Ambient:* hostile drones drift past her without targeting (one authored banter line
   hangs a lampshade: "they drift wrong lately"); 2. *Knowledge slips:* she opens a door /
   knows a patrol gap she was never taught (a `lua` condition can check actual game state
   for "she couldn't know this"); 3. *The voice:* camped at night, she speaks in a flat
   second register — the host plays one line from her `infected_lost` pool at whisper
   volume. Players who notice set `mole.suspected` via an interact; players who never
   notice get ambushes that feel unlucky (the hive shadowing the squad) and may learn the
   truth only in an ending slide. **The arc is entirely missable, by design.**

**Confrontation (the `carrier_confront` tree):** she offers her own knife grip-first and
asks you to read the map she can't. Three resolutions, all priced:
- **Cure path** (mercy/karma): the F6 synthesis lab / Salvari cure tech can purge a dormant
  strand — a fetch-and-defend quest; success clears `carrier`, raises rel to Bond, and
  banks toward the high endings.
- **Double agent** (`mole.weaponized`): keep the tapped wire and feed it lies — concrete
  payoffs (one guaranteed false-ambush you turn around; intel poisoning before a boss),
  but the strand *advances on every use*; push it too often and her `infected_lost`
  becomes live. Her conditions (watched sleep, Aria's checks, the agreed toll) are the
  scene's moral weight.
- **The knife:** on-camera, player-held, irreversible. Karma and the surviving girls'
  trust react hard (Keisha's promise-logic makes her the one who *understands*; Aria may
  not forgive it). This is the dark-timeline fuel.

**Canon fit.** Uses the existing infection/cure stage model (timeline.h `[0,90,60,30,0]`),
the existing victim→boss motif (her lost-state is just deferred), and gives the Act-2/3
Beta-boss machinery a fourth voice without adding a timeline. It also weaponizes the
rescue mechanic's own generosity: the player who heroically saves a girl at the last
second is the player who acquires the mole — *triage has aftershocks*.

## (d) THE ARCHITECT'S ROOM — the Club 1127 underworld arc (why a nightclub at the bottom of the world)

**Premise.** The club isn't an anomaly the lab tolerates; it's the oldest piece of the
site. The deep-bore crews that sank the original shafts (decades before "Lab Zero" had a
number) broke into the alien tube network — and their chief engineer, **the Architect**,
understood two things at once: that the thing below hums, and that the hum *listens*. He
built one room the building would never admit to, tuned its sound system a quarter-tone
off the substrate's resonance — **the speakers are a lullaby, pointed down** — and never
went back up. Vesper keeps it running. The PA rig canon (`x3-club1127.js`: four SVS subs,
the JBL stacks) is re-read as story: the club's bass is load-bearing.

**Why the Dominion tolerates it (the underworld logic):** three reasons, discoverable in
this order — (1) *pacification:* a staff pressure-valve makes the upstairs lie ("Nevada
desert") hold; (2) *dependency:* the freight line's human brokers (the Quartermaster's
people, thread a) need a neutral market, and Vesper's bar is the only neutral ground on
the rock; (3) the real one, sold last: **the Dominion's own instruments behave near the
substrate** — their drones drift, their telepathy fuzzes (the Illuminated won't descend
past −180). The club sits inside the one acoustic shadow the hive can't listen through.
The most surveilled building in two worlds has a confessional in its basement, and
*everyone* — staff, runners, collaborators, eventually Jake — needs it.

**The arc:** Act-1 tail: earn Vesper's rumor menu; the codes lore (1127 = the Architect's
door, **1278 = his cell** — he was eventually *processed by the program he dug the
foundations for*; the trapdoor room under the cell, already live via `secret_room.lua`,
is his: tools, drawings, and the third number). Mid-game: the **third room** rumor
(`rumor.third_room`) — deeper than the club, "his real work in it": an Architect-built
listening station at the Alien Substrate boundary (−400, canon-reachable per blueprint
§2.6), where his drawings reveal he wasn't hiding *from* the thing below. He was keeping
it asleep — and teaching the rock's hum to whoever came next. This hands Act 2's
underground biome a human ghost-story spine and gives the bio-mesh "substrate scan"
upgrade (HIDDEN_AREAS §4) a narrative father: the scan reads *his* markings.

**Canon fit.** Club position/contents untouched; explains its persistence as a hub across
all acts; binds the three numbers (1127/1278/the third) into one man's addresses; no
conflict with "Tim's real Miami club" provenance — in-fiction it's one builder's homesick
masterpiece.

## (e) THE BIOMESH SEA — an Act-2 thread that recontextualizes Act 1

**Premise.** Act 2's open world (desert, caves, maze, ocean, seafloor base) is currently a
*setting*. This thread makes it an *organism*. The alien tubes under the Spire, the
singing-crystal caves, the maze arteries at −315, and the trench past the undersea base
are one contiguous structure: the fossil **root system of the Original seeding pod** —
the 65-Myr artifact (canon, L45) is its heart, and most of it is dead. Most.

**The recontextualization, in three reveals:**
1. **"We weren't moving THROUGH something. We were moving INSIDE something."** (Lena's
   lost-state line — true.) Mid-Act-2, in the Crystal Heart chamber (canon L12), the
   geometry teaches it visually: the cave's "ruins predating the Overlord" aren't
   buildings. They're *anatomy*. The crystals are mail (Static's superstition) — memory
   nodes of the dead root, which is why they "hum back different depending on who's
   asking," and why they answer Salvari mourners: the Salvari's own cradle-world was
   seeded by the same network. The cautionary mirror gets a literal mirror.
2. **The ocean is the living part.** The Leviathan (canon submarine-combat boss; Static's
   confession) is not a native animal: it's the root system's immune cell, still on
   patrol after sixty-five million years. The drowned base was the lab's first attempt to
   tap the root directly; the root *responded*; the lab pretends the base never existed.
   This converts the undersea content from biome to escalation: every depth-meter is
   closer to the part of the world that's still awake.
3. **The Spire is a needle, and Act 1 happened inside a parasite.** End of Act 2 (Archives
   or Crystal Heart, pre-Storm-Runner): the facility's deepest foundations are *grafted
   into the root* — the Dominion built Lab Zero here to feed on the network: substrate
   resonance is what gestation tanks can't synthesize (thread b's bioreactor problem —
   the womb works *because life here is downstream of the seed network*; Keth'zar's root
   is the freshest). Suddenly Act 1 re-reads: the elevator's literal strata, the horror
   events on certain floors, the drones drifting near the club's shadow, the trapdoor
   tunnels — the building was never just a building. It's a tick on something's back,
   and the player spent seven floors climbing the tick.

**Forward hooks.** Act 3's Salvari Prime shows the same root, *fully harvested* — a
husk-world; the strategic stakes of Act 4 sharpen (Earth's root is the next freshest —
the invasion is a harvest crew, on schedule, per thread b). The Mirror set-piece gains a
reading: break the binary, find the third path — the roots remember everyone who ever
walked them.

**Canon fit.** Builds only on established pieces: literal strata (§2.6 resolved), Salvari
crystals (gap §2.5a), the Original Artifact (L45), the undersea base + Leviathan (systems
catalog), the Crystal Heart. Nothing moves; everything connects.

## (f) ENDING VARIANTS — karma × roster × romance overlays

These are **variants/epilogue overlays slotted inside the canonical 12** (timeline.h's
`Ending` enum unchanged) — they key on state the chat-tree pack generates: karma band,
which girls survived (`savedWomen`, Lena, carrier status), and romance flags.

**(f1) "LANTERNS" — overlay on Golden / Polyamorous Family (Endings 1, 10).**
Gate: karma ≥ +60 · all rescuable girls alive · carrier cured (or never occurred) ·
Martinez talked down or spared · the evidence package assembled (manifest + ledger +
batch records). The epilogue adds the **collaborator trials** (Martinez prosecuting,
Reyes testifying — "First name Felix. Middle name 'reluctantly.'") and closes at Club
1127 above ground: Vesper pours on a liberated Earth, the speakers finally pointed UP.
Romance state decorates, never gates: whoever Jake chose (or the found family) carries
the lanterns; Aria reads the forty-one names somewhere with sky — the pack's oldest
promise, kept on screen.

**(f2) "THE QUIET COMPANION" — overlay on Solo Victory / New Beginning (Endings 8, 12).**
Gate: exactly one companion alive at the finale · romance consummated with her · karma
−20…+40 (the scraped-through middle). The standard ending plays, then one extra scene
authored per girl in her own voice — Keisha names a daughter after no one and teaches
her to be loud; Emily publishes the memo with both authors' names on it; Aria runs a
clinic where nobody is ever a number; Lena charts a new world and marks one route
toll-free, forever. Cost surfaced gently: the scene names who *isn't* there. One
companion, fully seen, instead of a montage.

**(f3) "HOLLOW CROWN" — overlay on Dark (Ending 6).**
Gate: karma ≤ −40 · Humanity low (canon Dark gate) · at least one romance flag set **with**
negative-karma usage of her (the manipulative pattern: romance accepted, then her
sidequest refused / her trust scenes skipped / the double-agent strand spent ≥3 times).
The Dark ending's corruption beat gains its mirror: the companions are still alive, still
*his* — and the epilogue is the throne room nobody speaks in. Keisha stands post out of a
promise that no longer has a person on the other end; the last shot is Pete's line made
cosmic: he survived. He just arrived, alive, with nobody to drink with. (The carrier, if
weaponized and never cured, gets the final whisper — the hive thanking Jake, sincerely,
for the demonstration.)

**Canon fit.** No new endings, no new gates on the canonical table — overlays read state
the pack already writes, so they're pure epilogue content (slides/scenes), the cheapest
possible implementation for the highest replay-meaning yield.
